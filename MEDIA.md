## Blackpine photography direction

Photography must have a job. It should provide technical evidence, material texture, real environment, place, or scale. Decorative cyber imagery is not enough.

Preferred subject groups:

1. Technical infrastructure — server hardware, switching, cabling, storage, datacenter detail, controlled physical systems
2. Electronics and engineering — PCBs, connectors, silicon, test equipment, close component detail
3. Real technical work — hands, workspaces, hardware interaction, whiteboards, field documentation, engineering process
4. Industrial architecture — secure facilities, concrete, steel, technical corridors, controlled access, structural geometry
5. Canada / northern environment — restrained geographic context, overcast city/industrial landscape, forest, rock, cold water, winter materiality
6. Blackpine projects — Cipher UI, diagrams, test output, verification states, architecture and release material

The final corporate site should use a small, deliberate set of images rather than a large decorative library. A reasonable target is approximately 12–20 production images across the broader site, with many pages using typography, diagrams, or data instead of photography.

## Image treatment

- Prefer wide editorial crops over generic centred stock composition.
- Allow dark areas and negative space for typography when the source supports it.
- Avoid extreme teal colour grading. Blackpine green belongs in the interface, not painted over every photograph.
- Preserve believable material colour and lighting.
- Do not put a random image beside every section.
- Infrastructure photographs should show enough detail to feel real, not merely become an abstract blue texture.
- Forest imagery is an environmental signature and should appear sparingly.

## Reject list

Do not use:

- hooded hackers
- glowing padlocks or shields
- Matrix-style code rain
- anonymous people in blue server rooms
- neon cyber grids
- fake SOC dashboards
- generic world maps with glowing network arcs
- stock handshake imagery
- random office-laptop photography with no security context
- search-engine images without a verified rights record

Generated cyber imagery should generally be avoided on the corporate site. If generated visual material is ever used, it must have a specific technical or editorial purpose and must not be presented as documentary evidence.

## Future custom shot list

When Blackpine can produce original photography, prioritize:

- controlled workstation and hardware detail
- server/network equipment close-ups
- hands working with physical systems rather than posed portraits
- technical diagrams or notes in context
- project UI on real displays
- cable, rack, connector, board, and enclosure detail
- architectural exterior/interior with strong material geometry
- restrained Canadian environmental frames for company pages

## Rights policy

Do not add images from Google Images, social media, press articles, or commercial libraries without recording the source and rights. For every third-party asset retain the source URL, creator where available, stated license/terms, date selected, intended use, and production derivative information.

Real Blackpine photography and purpose-built technical diagrams take priority over stock.
