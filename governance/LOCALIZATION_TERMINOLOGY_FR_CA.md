# Blackpine Canadian-French Cybersecurity Terminology

Status: Phase 2 controlled editorial terminology

Locale: `fr-CA`

This glossary supports the Blackpine corporate-site localization. It is not a word-for-word translation table. Context, grammar, readability, and claim boundaries take precedence over mechanical substitution.

## Editorial register

Blackpine French should sound like professional Canadian cybersecurity writing: precise, restrained, direct, and technically literate. Prefer clear verbs and concrete nouns over promotional abstractions. Avoid translating English syntax literally. Avoid unnecessary anglicisms when established Canadian-French terminology exists.

Use `vous` when addressing the visitor. Keep Blackpine as the grammatical subject where that makes the sentence clearer than repeated impersonal constructions.

## Protected technical tokens

The following normally remain unchanged in French unless they are being explained: `DNS`, `MFA`, `SPF`, `DKIM`, `DMARC`, `TLS`, `API`, `CVE`, `ISO/IEC`, `NIST`, `CMMC`, `SOC 2`, `Microsoft 365`, `Google Workspace`, `Blackpine`, `Blackpine Cipher`.

Hostnames, email addresses, URLs, hashes, dates, version identifiers, personal names, legal entity names, and product names are not translated.

## Recurring Blackpine wording

- `Emergency Response` as a branded route may appear as **Intervention d’urgence** in French navigation and explanatory copy.
- `Investigate` → **Enquêter** or **Investigation** depending grammatical position; prefer the verb in problem-first navigation.
- `Recover` → **Rétablir** in problem-first navigation when the visitor is trying to regain control; use **reprise** for the formal incident-response phase.
- `Protect` → **Protéger**.
- `Monitor` → **Surveiller**.
- `Security Desk` → **Service de sécurité** in explanatory French; retain **Blackpine Security Desk** only when explicitly treating it as the branded service name.
- `Trust Center` → **Centre de confiance**.
- `Research` → **Recherche**.
- `Work` in the evidence/client-work section → **Réalisations** rather than the literal `Travail`.
- `Capabilities` → **Expertises** or **Services** depending context. Use **Expertises** for the main navigation architecture and **services spécialisés** where that is what the sentence means.

## Style checks before publication

Reject or rewrite copy that shows these machine-translation patterns:

- English noun stacks reproduced as long French noun chains;
- repeated `permettre de` where a direct verb is clearer;
- repeated `afin de` where `pour` is more natural;
- overuse of passive voice copied from English;
- `remédiation`, `monitoring`, `endpoint`, `pentest`, `threat intelligence`, or other avoidable anglicisms in general-facing prose;
- dropped qualifiers such as `peut`, `lorsque convenu`, `dans la portée`, `fictif`, or `non vérifié de façon indépendante`;
- stronger claims than the English source;
- France-specific wording where a standard Canadian-French cybersecurity term is established;
- inconsistent translations of the same technical concept without a contextual reason.

This glossary is an editorial control, not a substitute for sentence-level review.
