# Client Case Study Release Gate

## Purpose

Define the release checklist for any Blackpine client case study. This gate supplements the Client Evidence Publication Standard and prevents testimonial material from being expanded with plausible but unsupported engagement details.

## Required gates

A full anonymized case study requires all eight gates below to pass.

1. **Publication basis** — documented client permission or another clear publication basis.
2. **Safe organization description** — only approved sector, scale, geography or other identifying context.
3. **Problem** — the actual business/security problem without invented urgency, breach status or financial impact.
4. **Scope** — what was actually assessed or delivered, plus material exclusions where necessary for interpretation.
5. **Material evidence** — findings/observations approved for publication and traceable to the engagement evidence packet.
6. **Action taken** — remediation or decisions that actually occurred. Recommendations alone are not outcomes.
7. **Result** — evidence-supported outcome. Qualitative praise is not converted into percentages, ROI, savings or risk-reduction claims.
8. **Final review** — reviewer, approval date, confidentiality check, source-evidence references and correction owner recorded.

A named case study additionally requires explicit approval to name the organization and any quoted people.

## Automatic fail conditions

- invented client/company identity;
- invented client size, location, industry detail or architecture;
- invented incident/breach status;
- invented findings or tools used;
- invented remediation completion;
- invented percentages, ROI, savings or time reduction;
- customer logo without publication rights;
- confidential details exposed beyond the approval basis;
- testimonial-derived snapshot described as an independent case study.

## Withdrawal and correction

If the publication basis changes, a client requests an appropriate correction/withdrawal, or the material becomes inaccurate, Blackpine must update or retire the public case study rather than preserve stale credibility evidence.
